Gibbons are some of the most expressive animals I have ever seen, and in Thailand many of them are in trouble. Some are taken from the wild as babies to be kept as pets or used for tourist photos, and they often struggle to survive on their own afterward. Rehabilitation centers give these gibbons a chance to recover and, where possible, return to the forest.

## Why I got involved

Living in Thailand has shown me how closely wildlife and everyday life exist side by side here. My interest in gibbons started as curiosity and grew into something I care about deeply. Today I am the team leader of the Rescuing Gibbons Foundation, and this is the project I am most proud of.

## What I do

As team leader, I organize a group of students, especially Grade 11 students, to volunteer at the sanctuary every weekend. I schedule who comes, make sure everyone knows their tasks, and keep the team on track.

Each session is about three hours of hands-on work. We clean up the gibbons' feces and scrub their cages, and we aim to finish at least two cages every time. It is not glamorous work, but a clean and healthy enclosure matters a lot for the animals' recovery.

Sometimes the work is even heavier. When a floor needs to be rebuilt, we mix cement by hand. That means carrying around 60 kg of cement and sand, mixing it together, and laying the new floor. It is tiring, but seeing the finished result makes it worth it.

Leading a team through this has taught me how to listen, stay patient, and keep going even when the work is hard.
